LexSR: Consolidating Legislation and Linking Case Law to Build a Legal Knowledge Graph for Suriname, a Low-Resource Jurisdiction

==============================================================================
AUTHOR

==============================================================================
ABSTRACT  (256 words, 1841 characters)
==============================================================================
Access to consolidated, machine-readable legislation and linked case law is a prerequisite for effective legal information systems. No such resource previously existed for Suriname, a Dutch-speaking jurisdiction of fewer than 600,000 people with a predominantly scanned legal archive. We present LexSR, a pipeline that ingests raw government PDFs and the Hof van Justitie's case-law portal, applies OCR, classifies documents, automatically consolidates wijzigingswetten (amendment acts) into versioned legislative expressions modelled on FRBR, and links case law to the legislation it applies. The pipeline produces a corpus of 1,623 works (1,323 legislative, 300 judicial) and 45,293 fragments, from which we construct a unified knowledge graph of 2,768 edges spanning amendment, citation, and case-application relationships. We report two evaluations: a synthetic citation-resolution benchmark, where our resolver achieves 100% precision/recall against a curated 61-case gold set, and, as the paper's methodological contribution, a measurement of how much of that resolution accuracy actually survives contact with the real, messily-titled corpus it was built to run against. Only 17% of extracted citations resolve to an in-corpus work. We attribute this to a structural mismatch between the idealized canonical identifiers assumed by curated benchmarks and the filename-derived identifiers that scraped, OCR'd ingestion actually produces, and show that reconciling the two recovers most of the shortfall. We also measure amendment-parsing coverage as the corpus grew from 33 to 50 amendment documents (82% to 38%), a second instance of the same divergence between curated-subset accuracy and real-corpus performance. We discuss implications for low-resource legal NLP and make the corpus, code, and evaluation scripts publicly available.
